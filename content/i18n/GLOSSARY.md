# English translation: glossary and rules

The French copy is the reference (calibrated for SEO by the agency). The English is a faithful professional translation for a B2B audience (facility managers, office managers, hotel managers, property managers). Everything must be reviewed by the client before publication.

## Rules

1. **British English** (organise, colour, centre, metre…).
2. **Translate meaning, not word for word.** Keep every fact, figure, place and condition. Add nothing, invent nothing, drop nothing.
3. **No prices, no « Végétal Office »**, no em dash (—) used as a connector. Use a colon, comma or full stop instead.
4. Quotes: French « … » become English “…”.
5. **Keep as is:** Flora Concept, FLORACONCEPT, SIREN/VAT numbers, addresses, phone numbers, URLs, place names (Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, Île-de-France, Gémenos, Aix-en-Provence, Sophia Antipolis, La Défense, Hauts-de-Seine, Luberon…), m², 1999.
6. **Segments:** each entry is one piece of a sentence. When neighbouring entries belong to the same sentence (for example, text, then link text, then text), translate them so that, joined in order, they still read as one correct English sentence. Keep leading/trailing spaces and punctuation at the same boundaries.
7. Headings stay short and punchy. Button labels stay imperative.
8. Legal pages: precise, formal register; keep French legal references (RCS, RGPD → GDPR, CNIL, loi/article numbers) recognisable.
